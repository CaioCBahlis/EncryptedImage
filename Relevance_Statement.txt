Relevance Statement:
This project applies fundamental concepts from Part I (Secret Key Cryptography) of our MAD 4472 course and the course textbook, Boneh and Shoup's A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography, to break a symmetric encryption scheme using a known-plaintext attack. In Chapter 2 (Encryption: Shannon Ciphers, Perfect Security, and Semantic Security), we learn that perfect secrecy requires a key at least as long as the message and never reused, and that a semantically secure cipher must leak no information about the plaintext. In enc27-1-1.py, however, the plaintext PNG file is padded to a multiple of 24 bytes and encrypted block-by-block by XORing every 24-byte block with the same 24-byte key. This directly mirrors two classic vulnerabilities studied in class: key reuse across blocks, which reduces the scheme to an insecure many-time pad (Chapter 3, Section 3.3.1: "The two-time pad is insecure"), and deterministic block-by-block encryption analogous to Electronic Codebook (ECB) mode (Chapter 4, Section 4.1.4, and our Week 2 Block Cipher exercises). Because bitwise XOR is linear and self-inverse (K = P XOR C), knowing the fixed 16-byte PNG signature and IHDR header immediately leaks the first 16 key bytes. Combining those 16 recovered bytes with the deterministic PKCS#7-style padding appended by add_pad (related to block-cipher padding concepts in Chapter 5) and the fixed 12-byte PNG IEND trailer uniquely identifies the padding length L and reveals the final 8 key bytes—recovering the entire 192-bit key in Maple without brute-forcing the 2^192 keyspace.
